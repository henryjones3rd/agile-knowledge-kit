# Observe first

When you are assigned to a new team, resist the urge to roll out practices on
day one. Understand the team before you try to change it.

## The four phases

1. **Observe.** Attend every ceremony and working session for the first few
   weeks. Take notes on how work actually arrives, moves, and finishes.
2. **Help in the here and now.** Remove the blocker in front of the team today.
   Earning trust comes before proposing change.
3. **Ask questions.** Two questions reveal the most:
    - What value does this team create for others?
    - Why does working with this team feel slow to the people who depend on it?
4. **Present gradually.** Share observations first, then evidence, then
   proposed changes. Teams adopt what they help shape.

## What to write down

| Look for | Example signal |
| --- | --- |
| How work arrives | Most requests come as chat messages, not backlog items |
| Where work waits | Reviews sit for days before anyone picks them up |
| Repeated effort | Every request starts from scratch, even standard ones |
| Unowned problems | An issue everyone sees but nobody is assigned to fix |

## Why it works

Jumping straight to "better sprints" can make a reactive team more efficient at
being reactive. Observing first shows you the problem actually worth solving.

# Relative estimation

A story point measures how big a piece of work is **compared with other work
the team has done**. It is not a unit of time.

## The common failure

Teams often define "1 point = 1 day." Once that happens:

- Estimates turn into promises about hours.
- People pad estimates to protect themselves.
- Velocity stops being a forecasting tool and becomes a performance score.

## How to estimate relatively

1. Pick two or three **reference stories** the whole team remembers: one small,
   one medium, one large.
2. For each new item, ask "Is this bigger or smaller than the reference?"
3. Use a coarse scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13). Gaps between numbers reflect growing
   uncertainty.
4. Split anything larger than 13 before committing to it.

## Coaching script

When someone says "That's about three days," reply:

> "Compared with the login story we did last month, is it bigger or smaller?"

Redirect to comparison every time. The habit changes within a few sprints.

## Epics and T-shirt sizes

For quarterly planning, size epics with T-shirt sizes (S, M, L, XL) and map each
size to a point range only after the team agrees on reference epics.
